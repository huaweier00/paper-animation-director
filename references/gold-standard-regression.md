# Gold-standard regression review

Use this reference before approving the performance benchmark and again before final picture lock. Its purpose is not to copy an earlier film's style. It protects the qualities that made the strongest productions work: specific intention, physical causality, shot-shaped rhythm, complete silhouettes, and project identity.

## 1. Review protocol

Compare an active passage with the closest gold passage in three synchronized views:

1. silent playback at normal speed;
2. normal playback with sound;
3. six to twelve proof frames covering preparation, action, contact, consequence, reaction, and exit.

Do not compare isolated beauty frames alone. A stronger still can hide weaker acting.

Write no more than five observations:

- the active passage's strongest choice;
- the first moment attention becomes unclear;
- the weakest physical or emotional transition;
- one quality in which it exceeds the gold passage;
- the single highest-value revision.

Revise the shot, not the scorecard. Do not add a new schema or validator because a creative judgment is difficult.

## 3. Gold standard A — Three Monks

The reference production is a roughly 68-second, six-scene cutout/painterly paper film. Its transferable strength is economy: each scene has one changed story state, each prop movement belongs to a cause, and repeated actions escalate instead of looping mechanically.

### A1. Solo water carry

Observe:

- a connected walk cycle rather than independently floating limbs;
- pole flex, bucket lag, water counter-motion, step impact, and ground shadow supporting the same weight;
- scenery and camera motion remaining subordinate to the carrying action;
- the scene leaving soon after the daily labor is established.

Regression signs:

- the actor translates while the body does not carry weight;
- water and bucket move in phase with the torso;
- particles, parallax, or camera drift become more noticeable than the walk;
- the scene continues after its fact is understood.

### A2. Two-person carry

Observe:

- dispute changing into a shared load;
- two bodies agreeing on one pole, bucket, direction, and ground plane;
- a readable compression of the pole and lag of the bucket;
- the carried object organizing the ensemble instead of two isolated actors being moved together.

Regression signs:

- hands nearly touch the pole but never own it;
- actors move at identical amplitude and phase regardless of height or effort;
- the shared prop passes through bodies or has no response to acceleration;
- narration explains cooperation that the pose does not show.

### A3. Three-person refusal

Observe:

- gaze and gesture forming a triangle of blame;
- three distinct statuses and rhythms rather than three copies;
- the water level quietly changing the material stakes while the argument continues;
- the scene cutting once the empty-jar consequence is legible.

Regression signs:

- everyone waves or bobs at the same tempo;
- the empty vessel appears without being connected to prolonged refusal;
- the camera supplies energy while the social relation stays static;
- the argument holds after the consequence has landed.

### A4. Lamp fall and fire spread

Observe the causal chain:

```text
lamp instability
→ fall and impact
→ oil path
→ ground flame
→ structure catches
→ main fire grows
→ characters notice in sequence
→ they seek water
→ the empty jar becomes a consequence of earlier refusal
```

The scene succeeds because danger does not appear as a replacement texture. It travels through objects and decisions.

Regression signs:

- the full fire fades in before a cause is visible;
- lamp, flame, smoke, glow, and oil have unrelated anchors;
- all characters enter with the same reaction at the same time;
- the empty jar is shown before the fire has created a need for water;
- fire loops for spectacle after the decision to respond is clear.

### A5. Bucket line

Observe:

- each bucket having an owner, route, handoff, pour, impact, steam response, and fire consequence;
- the next bucket beginning only when the previous action has cleared enough visual space;
- first, second, and third impacts producing different fire states;
- actors accumulating effort and changing balance across repetitions;
- sound marking contacts and impacts rather than merely filling the sequence.

Regression signs:

- buckets travel as decorative projectiles;
- water begins before the bucket tips;
- every impact uses the same pose, timing, splash, and result;
- fire is already extinguished before the final effort;
- the sequence is busy but no handoff can be followed.

### A6. Morning resolution

Observe:

- labor changing from emergency cooperation to sustainable routine;
- different actors taking turns rather than a generic group pose claiming cooperation;
- separate pours entering the jar and producing distinct water responses;
- light, spacing, and tempo resolving the social relationship without excessive moral text;
- the final hold arriving only after the changed behavior is visible.

Regression signs:

- a full jar appears as an unexplained symbol;
- all three characters merely stand together;
- the moral statement arrives before behavior proves it;
- sunrise, birds, and paper texture carry more meaning than the actors.

## 4. Gold standard B — Shadow Theatre Origin

The reference production is a horizontal historical shadow-theatre film. Its transferable strength is spatial proof: bed, curtain, lamp, screen, performer, shadow, observer, and blocking actor occupy readable relations. Light behavior is part of the drama, not a decorative filter.

### B1. Bedchamber refusal

Observe:

- the room reading immediately as a maintained, high-status historical interior rather than a generic dark room;
- bed, canopy, curtain opening, floor, lamp, and both actors sharing perspective and scale;
- the emperor approaching, stopping before the curtain, and withdrawing on the same ground plane;
- the lady's refusal changing the whole body-and-bedding state rather than moving a detached hand;
- curtain and flame reactions following the internal action in sequence;
- captions preserving the hand, curtain edge, bedding, feet, and proof silhouette.

Regression signs:

- camera movement reveals the room only after the action has begun;
- the lamp flame floats separately from the lamp;
- a pose swap changes actor scale or ground contact;
- the curtain deforms as a flat UI panel with no fixed point or weight;
- narration states refusal while the picture only shows two held portraits.

### B2. Conscious speech

Observe:

- a quiet scene using a whole-tableau state change to preserve bed, body, bedding, and support-hand contact;
- sisters facing the bed as a connected group rather than facing the viewer;
- small lighting changes punctuating thought without replacing acting;
- negative space supporting refusal and mortality;
- the camera remaining restrained because the dialogue is the pressure.

Regression signs:

- grief represented by generic tears, floating icons, or exaggerated camera motion;
- the seated state losing connection to bed and bedding;
- every clause receiving a new visual effect;
- stillness being padded by scale breathing or particles.

### B3. Screen, performer, lamp, observer

Observe the depth chain:

```text
rear lamp
→ articulated performer
→ screen plane
→ projected shadow
→ observer
```

The screen is an object in space. The shadow is an optical result. Moving the performer toward or away from the screen changes edge sharpness, size, offset, and intensity coherently.

Regression signs:

- an opaque painted actor is placed on the audience side and called a shadow;
- the shadow moves without a performer or light cause;
- size changes but softness and offset do not;
- the screen behaves like a glowing web panel;
- rods and joints are displayed constantly even when they distract from the story.

### B4. Approach, interception, near-loss of the shadow

Observe:

- desire beginning before locomotion;
- the emperor rising and advancing with a complete weight shift;
- the blocking actor intercepting with a connected two-person contact state;
- curtain or gauze responding to the interrupted momentum;
- the performer moving off the optimal plane so the shadow enlarges, softens, and shifts;
- the hold allowing the audience to understand what nearly disappeared;
- recovery restoring light, performer, screen, and observers to a stable relation.

Regression signs:

- two separate PNGs slide together without a credible stop;
- the blocker has no stance or counter-force;
- the shadow becomes blurry as an arbitrary transition;
- the physical danger is understandable only from narration;
- the sequence resolves before the viewer can read the optical consequence.

### B5. Palace-to-folk legacy transition

Observe:

- sound leading the historical transition;
- court language yielding to a public stage through light and performance rather than an information card;
- a new folk puppet making the tradition feel alive rather than archived;
- child spectators reacting as a connected audience;
- the final statement landing after the craft's continuation is visible.

Regression signs:

- the ending becomes a slide of facts, dates, badges, or calls to action;
- the same palace image is reused with a different caption;
- historical significance is narrated but not embodied by a living performance;
- the film continues after its emotional and visual conclusion.

## 5. Comparison scorecard

Use a 1–5 scale. Scores organize discussion; they do not replace judgment.

| Dimension | 1 | 3 | 5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dramatic responsibility | No visible changed state | State change is readable with narration | Silent viewer reads desire, action, consequence, and new state |
| Character intention | Actor merely moves | Broad emotion or task is readable | Thought, hesitation, status, and decision shape timing and pose |
| Contact and force | Floating, sliding, or false touch | Contact exists but response is generic | Force travels through bodies, props, ground, material, and reaction |
| Repetition and escalation | Identical loop | Minor variation | Each repetition changes effort, timing, result, or relationship |
| Spatial coherence | Scale and depth are unstable | Basic ground and target are plausible | Perspective, occlusion, screen direction, light, and contact reinforce story |
| Visual identity | Generic template look | Medium is recognizable | Silhouette, palette, edge, negative space, and typography belong only to this project |
| Medium service | Medium is false or cosmetic | Medium is technically credible | Medium creates dramatic possibilities without demanding attention in every shot |
| Rhythm and editing | Dead holds or restless activity | Functional pacing | Preparation, impact, hold, reaction, silence, and cut form an authored phrase |
| Sound-action relation | Sound is absent or wallpaper | Major events have cues | Timbre, distance, hierarchy, silence, and timing strengthen force and emotion |
| Economy | Effects compete with story | Some redundant motion | Every visible or audible choice supports the shot's responsibility |

Do not average away a fatal weakness. Silent readability, contact/force, visual identity, and rhythm must each score at least 3. The active benchmark should equal or exceed the closest gold passage in at least one dimension; otherwise it is only reproducing a weaker version of known work.

## 6. Regression decisions

Choose one:

- **advance** — no fatal weakness; the passage has its own strength and is ready to expand;
- **revise one problem** — preserve the strongest choice and repair the highest-value weakness;
- **change architecture** — the assets or implementation cannot express the approved performance;
- **change visual direction** — the result remains generic despite competent execution;
- **shorten or remove** — the shot has completed its responsibility or never earned its duration;
- **return to beats** — no implementation can rescue an unclear story change.

Do not respond to a weak comparison by adding layers, particles, camera travel, schemas, engines, or review fields.

## 7. What not to copy

Gold standards preserve quality principles, not surface style. Do not automatically copy:

- watercolor, warm rice paper, dark-brown captions, temple imagery, Han interiors, or folk-stage motifs;
- six-scene or eleven-scene structures;
- the exact camera moves, subtitle design, palette, voice, or transitions;
- complete-pose replacement when a different approved medium needs another method;
- the historical productions' known warnings, compromises, or unfinished ambitions.

The new film must be recognizably itself. A project that imitates the gold standard's appearance while losing its causal precision is a regression.
