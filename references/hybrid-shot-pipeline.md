# Hybrid shot pipeline

Use this reference after `animation-decision.json` is approved and before generating shot assets. It converts a directing decision into a capability profile, an engine plan, an asset contract, and a deterministic HyperFrames composition.

## 1. Architecture

HyperFrames remains the orchestrator. It owns:

- the top-level timeline;
- shot timing and mounting;
- narration, dialogue, music, sound effects, and captions;
- global overlays, transitions, and watermark;
- deterministic frame capture, final encoding, validation, and delivery.

Specialized engines own only the layer or shot capability they are selected for:

| Engine | Owns | Does not own |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `gsap-dom` | typography, editorial overlays, rigid paper layers, simple parallax | skeletal acting, mesh deformation, simulation |
| `rive` | reusable 2D bones, IK, mesh deformation, linear character animation | global edit, audio, subtitles, stateful runtime story logic |
| `spine` | reusable production 2D rigs, constraints, weighted meshes, animation mixing | global edit, audio, subtitles |
| `pixijs-webgpu` | deterministic 2D particles, displacement, masks, filters, ink, dust, snow, water | character identity and story timing |
| `three-webgpu` | 2.5D/3D space, camera, lighting, spatial particles, shaders | narration, captions, global edit |
| `blender` | authored 3D rigs, cloth, hair, physical contact, stateful simulations, complex hero shots | final film assembly |

Do not select an engine because it is fashionable. Select it only when the shot has a capability that the simpler route cannot credibly prove.

## 2. The two routing artifacts

### `shot-capabilities.json`

This is an authored statement of objective shot requirements. It describes the motion representation, contact, depth, camera, particles, simulation, typography, reuse, constraints, and rendered proof. It must not name an engine except in optional preferences.

Start from:

`assets/project-template/manifests/shot-capabilities.example.json`

### `engine-plan.json`

This is generated by the router. It records:

- selected engines and their roles;
- runtime or pre-render integration;
- selection reasons;
- risk score and benchmark requirement;
- the absolute-time seek contract;
- fallback paths;
- engine-specific asset requirements;
- rendered proof requirements.

Do not hand-edit a generated plan to hide a routing problem. Change the capability profile or an explicit preference, rerun the router, and preserve both files.

## 3. Capability vocabulary

### Character motion

- `none` — no independently animated character.
- `rigid-2d` — a complete connected figure moves as one rigid layer.
- `pose-replacement` — complete authored poses replace one another; no continuous joint interpolation is claimed.
- `skeletal-2d` — continuous joints, IK, or reusable gait/performance.
- `mesh-deform-2d` — raster or vector art must bend, squash, ripple, or preserve continuous soft anatomy/clothing.
- `3d-rig` — the shot needs an authored 3D skeleton, 3D deformation, or a 3D character-camera relationship.

### Contact

- `none` — no critical support or interaction.
- `staged` — contact can be authored as fixed poses or clearly separated states.
- `continuous` — hand, foot, hoof, rope, load, or body contact must remain credible through motion.
- `simulated` — collision, falling, cloth, secondary dynamics, or physical feedback must be solved or baked.

### Spatial depth

- `flat` — one integrated plane or graphic layout.
- `multiplane` — deterministic 2D layers with parallax.
- `2.5d` — paper/image planes live in a perspective scene with a real camera.
- `3d` — volumetric geometry, 3D rigs, or genuine 3D staging.

### Camera

- `static` — locked frame.
- `2d` — flat pan, push, zoom, or reframe.
- `parallax` — different depth planes move at different rates.
- `3d` — perspective camera translation, rotation, orbit, focus, or lighting relationship.

### Particles and simulation

- `particle_load`: `none`, `light`, or `heavy`.
- `simulation`: `none`, `deterministic-runtime`, or `baked-physics`.

Use `deterministic-runtime` only when the full state at time `t` is computable directly from `t`, fixed inputs, and fixed seeds. If a result depends on running frames `0…t` in order, classify it as `baked-physics`.

## 4. Engine selection rules

The router applies these principles:

1. Baked physics, simulated contact, or a 3D rig routes to Blender and returns a pre-rendered layer or full shot.
2. Skeletal or mesh-deformed 2D characters route to Rive by default; Spine is an explicit alternative.
3. Flat or multiplane 2D effects route to PixiJS.
4. A 2.5D/3D space or 3D camera routes to Three.js WebGPU.
5. If Three.js already owns spatial depth, heavy spatial particles stay in the same renderer instead of adding PixiJS.
6. Typography and editorial overlays stay in GSAP/DOM even when another engine owns the picture.
7. A quiet tableau with no specialized requirement stays in GSAP/DOM.

Route the smallest independently timed and reviewable production shot, not
necessarily the whole story scene. If a long editorial scene contains a quiet
approach, a short physical impact, and a quiet result, split it at those
capability boundaries:

```text
approach: Rive + PixiJS
impact: Blender pre-render
result: GSAP/DOM
→ one HyperFrames editorial scene
```

The split must preserve narration, screen direction, camera units, scale,
lighting, entry/exit pose, prop ownership, and contact state across boundaries.
Do not split merely to add engines; split when it confines an expensive or
stateful capability to the beat that actually needs it.

One shot may legitimately use several engines:

```text
Rive character
  + PixiJS dust
  + GSAP captions
  → HyperFrames shot
```

A complex hero shot normally collapses instead:

```text
Blender character + cloth + collision + camera
  → transparent or opaque pre-render
  + GSAP captions
  → HyperFrames shot
```

Avoid adding both PixiJS and Three.js when one renderer can own the required particles and spatial effects. Avoid adding a live character runtime when Blender already rendered the complete character performance.

## 5. Deterministic integration contract

Every embedded engine must accept absolute HyperFrames time:

```text
renderAt(localTimeSeconds, globalTimeSeconds)
```

Use `assets/project-template/runtime/hybrid-runtime.js` to register custom renderers. It listens for `hf-seek`, converts global time to shot-local time, removes disconnected renderers, and provides deterministic seeded values.

Mandatory rules:

- Do not use `requestAnimationFrame` as render truth.
- Do not use `Date.now()` or `performance.now()`.
- Do not accumulate simulation state frame by frame.
- Do not use unseeded `Math.random()`.
- Do not fetch render-critical assets at seek time.
- Keep engine assets local and versioned.
- Pin canvas dimensions and pixel ratio.
- Give every custom canvas a production fallback.
- Audit DOM/GSAP layers as rigorously as embedded canvases. A `fromTo` start
  state or transform cache can retain seek history even when the visual
  difference is only text antialiasing. Establish explicit states at timeline
  zero; for captions, prefer opacity-only transitions unless shuffled capture
  proves transform-based motion is byte-stable.

Rive state machines and other input-history-dependent graphs are not automatically seekable. Use a finite linear animation that can be positioned at absolute time, or pre-render it. Do not claim a state machine is deterministic merely because it plays consistently in a live preview.

Blender physics, cloth, hair, rigid bodies, and other history-dependent simulations must be baked. Preserve the editable source and baked caches, then export a transparent or opaque master for HyperFrames.

## 6. New-project workflow

Initialize the story project:

```bash
python3 scripts/init_paper_project.py \
  --manifest story-manifest.json \
  --output ./my-paper-story
```

The project receives:

- `hybrid-pipeline.json`;
- exact `package.json` and `package-lock.json` versions plus
  `offline-dependency-policy.json`;
- the complete `assets/runtime/` tree, including the shared bridge and verified
  PixiJS, Rive and Three.js adapters;
- portable production controllers under `tools/paper-pipeline/`;
- `engine-plans/`;
- per-shot asset and review directories created on demand;
- the ordinary HyperFrames project and story files.

For each production shot:

1. Approve the semantic event and spatial contract.
2. Approve `animation-decision.json`.
3. Copy and fill `shot-capabilities.example.json`.
4. Route it:

```bash
python3 scripts/route_shot_capabilities.py \
  shots/scene-01/shot-capabilities.json \
  --config hybrid-pipeline.json \
  --output shots/scene-01/engine-plan.json \
  --strict
```

5. Review the plan and its asset requirements.
6. Scaffold only the approved shot:

```bash
python3 scripts/scaffold_hybrid_shot.py \
  --plan shots/scene-01/engine-plan.json \
  --project .
```

7. Edit the generated `shots/<shot-id>/engine-inputs.json`. PixiJS and the
   bundled Three.js 2.5D template mount immediately; Rive, Spine and Blender
   remain behind explicit project-asset gates.
8. Author or supply the approved engine-shaped assets, clear every asset gate,
   and build/review the representative benchmark before expanding production.

Use the controller instead of manually replaying those commands:

```bash
python3 tools/paper-pipeline/build_routed_shot.py \
  --project . \
  --shot-id scene-01 \
  --phase prepare
```

The controller supports three guarded phases:

| Phase | Required result |
| --- | --- |
| `prepare` | planning decision passes; capabilities route strictly; safe scaffold completes; schema-v2 sidecars are created; development engine and ready-sidecar audits pass |
| `verify` | plan has not drifted; every selected engine and sidecar is release-ready; doctor, WebGPU probe when applicable, HyperFrames check, ordered/shuffled PNG comparison, and performance budget pass |
| `release` | all verify gates plus rendered animation responsibility and schema-v2 shot release pass |

Run `prepare` before engine-specific asset authoring. Run `verify` only after
replacing every authored-asset gate. Run `release` only after the real MP4,
muted/voice/combined reads, MP4 proof frames, and release record exist.

The scaffolder may replace a known development placeholder. It refuses to overwrite an authored composition unless `--force` is supplied. Inspect the existing file before ever using `--force`.
It copies the full versioned runtime tree and does not overwrite an existing
`engine-inputs.json`.

## 7. Per-shot implementation workflow

Keep this order:

```text
responsibility
→ spatial contract
→ animation decision
→ capability profile
→ engine plan
→ engine-shaped asset plan
→ benchmark implementation
→ deterministic checks
→ rendered proof
→ release
```

The engine plan changes asset generation:

- A Rive/Spine route needs separated or mesh-ready source art, not only full-scene PNGs.
- A Pixi route needs local textures, masks, emitter origins, fixed seeds, and effect bounds.
- A Three route needs depth plates or models, units, camera, textures, light, and shader inputs.
- A Blender route needs editable source, rigs, caches, render settings, color management, and output codec/alpha policy.
- A GSAP route may use complete connected poses, rigid layers, SVG, text, and deterministic local transforms.

Do not generate a full inventory for all engines. Generate only the assets required by the accepted route for the active shot.

## 8. Engine-specific build contracts

For the runnable adapters, exact package versions, asset-gate fields, commands,
and benchmark directories, read `references/engine-execution-templates.md`.

### Rive

- Use approved identity-conditioned art.
- Separate at natural joints or build a deformable mesh; protect silhouette and costume continuity.
- Author bones, IK, weights, and finite linear animations.
- Drive the linear animation from absolute local time.
- Pre-render any state machine whose result depends on input history.
- Review hand, foot, face, sleeve, hem, and prop contact at proof times.

### Spine

- Preserve the Spine project, atlas, skeleton data, and runtime version.
- Use weighted meshes and constraints only where they improve the declared action.
- Set animation time directly; do not rely on a free-running update loop.
- Pre-render physics or runtime behavior that cannot seek independently.

### PixiJS

- Create particle positions from fixed seeds.
- Derive position, rotation, scale, and opacity from absolute time and lifetime.
- Keep effect origin attached to the declared causal point.
- Bound filter areas and masks.
- Avoid feedback buffers unless they can be reconstructed from time; otherwise bake the effect.

### Three.js WebGPU

- Use the HyperFrames `hf-seek` event or the shared hybrid bridge.
- Set mixers with absolute time.
- Derive camera, shader uniforms, and seeded particles from absolute time.
- Pin renderer dimensions and pixel ratio.
- Use local textures, models, and HDRIs.
- Bake temporal accumulation, fluid feedback, or other previous-frame effects.

### Blender

- Lock scale, axes, camera, frame rate, color management, and render range.
- Bake simulations before review.
- Render handles only when the edit requires them.
- Export alpha only when later compositing needs it.
- Preserve `.blend`, external assets, caches, and render settings.
- Review the rendered output inside the final HyperFrames composite, not only in Blender.

## 9. Benchmark and release gates

`engine-plan.json` sets `benchmark_required` when the shot uses a specialized engine or has sufficient physical risk.

The benchmark must prove the selected capability, not merely prove that the engine loads:

- Rive/Spine: continuous joints, contact, deformation, and identity.
- PixiJS: effect origin, lifecycle, scale, masking, and deterministic seek.
- Three.js: camera, depth, occlusion, light, and deterministic seek.
- Blender: physical action, simulation, render quality, alpha/composite behavior, and edit timing.

Seek each embedded shot in non-sequential order:

```text
0.0 → midpoint → proof → 0.2 → final → midpoint
```

The same timestamp must produce the same pixels. Treat a mismatch as a render-blocking defect.

Release still requires:

- muted responsibility proof;
- voice-only proof;
- combined picture/sound/subtitle proof;
- HyperFrames checks and final-MP4 frame review;
- a schema-version-5 shot-release record that binds both routing artifacts plus the approved medium, performance, audio, motion, and rendered-review evidence
  plus `engine-inputs.json`, and passes `engine_plan_fulfilled` and
  `deterministic_seek`.

`audit_engine_inputs.py --phase release` is not a schema-only check. It rejects:

- an engine configured but not selected, or selected but not configured;
- runtime-network or data-URL assets;
- unseeded or malformed deterministic effects;
- missing Rive, Spine, Three.js, or Blender assets;
- a pending `ready: false` gate;
- Blender media whose path still names a placeholder or pending file;
- unbaked Blender physics, missing editable `.blend`, or missing pre-render manifest.

The schema-v2 release auditor calls this engine-input audit automatically.

## 10. Migrating an existing project

Do not re-route every quiet shot. Triage existing shots:

1. Keep deliberate stills, reaction shots, establishing views, typography, and simple parallax on GSAP/DOM when they already fulfil their responsibility.
2. Route only shots whose current representation cannot prove the required action or relationship.
3. Start with the hardest representative failure.
4. Preserve timing and narration while replacing only the picture layer.
5. Compare the revised rendered MP4 to the original at identical proof times.

Historical release records without `schema_version` are treated as legacy v1
and remain auditable with a migration warning. When a legacy shot is revised,
replace its release record with the v2 template and add the two routing
artifacts; do not silently grandfather newly rendered work.

Typical upgrade signals:

- whole-image zoom is standing in for a character action;
- several full-body state PNGs visibly jump between poses;
- feet, hooves, hands, ropes, or loads lose contact;
- cloth, hair, water, fire, dust, or smoke behaves as decoration rather than a causal effect;
- a flat camera move is standing in for needed spatial depth;
- a simulation is approximated by manually moved shapes and remains unreadable.

The goal is not to maximize the number of engines. The goal is to use the least complex route that can produce credible, inspectable evidence for the shot.

## 11. P2 route-aware production gates

New scaffolds use `engine-inputs.json` schema version 2. In addition to adapter
configuration, they carry engine-specific sidecars and a per-shot performance
budget. Read `references/p2-engine-capabilities.md` for the exact contracts and
commands.

The one-command controller owns their execution:

```text
prepare
  → engine-input development audit
  → ready Rive/Three/Blender sidecar audits
  → doctor sidecar-completeness check

verify/release
  → release engine-input audit
  → Rive asset/inspection SHA match
  → Three scene/occlusion audit
  → Blender action-library audit
  → real-device WebGPU probe when Three is selected
  → HyperFrames and deterministic-seek proof
  → mounted-renderer inventory and p95 budget
```

Legacy schema-version-1 shots remain supported. Migrate them deliberately; do
not silently add production gates to an already released shot.
