# Articulated cutout-paper route

Use this route for opaque paper, card, collage, pinned-joint, folded, torn, layered, or tabletop paper performers whose material construction remains visible.

## Contents

1. Medium truth
2. Performer architectures
3. Material and depth
4. Motion language
5. Sound and release gates

## 1. Medium truth

Declare paper/card type, thickness, edge treatment, print/paint method, joint/pin visibility, fold behavior, shadow behavior, and depth model. Paper construction must affect design or motion; a generic raster illustration under a paper texture belongs in the painterly-limited route.

Do not fake richness with uniform fibers, torn rims, drop shadows, curled corners, particles, or parallax. Use only material traits that serve the selected construction.

## 2. Performer architectures

Choose from:

- articulated parts with named pivots, hidden overlaps, draw order, and support points;
- complete replacement poses when articulation would expose seams or look mechanical;
- connected ensemble states for touch, lift, carry, embrace, restraint, handoff, or shared load;
- integrated scene states for bedding, cloth, water, rope, or fragile perspective;
- deliberate still tableau with earned-stillness evidence.

Record the choice in `performance-contract.json`. Do not cut a hero into parts merely because the route permits joints.

## 3. Material and depth

Prove material scale and contact:

- edges match source resolution and intended display size;
- cast/contact shadows correspond to layer height and light direction;
- pivots remain covered through the approved range;
- feet, wheels, props, and placed objects remain attached to support surfaces;
- foreground occlusion follows the declared tabletop/multiplane depth;
- texture scale stays consistent between character, prop, and environment.

## 4. Motion language

Paper motion may be stepped, crisp, tactile, or deliberately mechanical. It still requires intention, anticipation, action, contact/change, hold, settle, and reaction where applicable.

Reject:

- neutral full-body PNG translation used as walking or acting;
- every part rotating with the same easing and phase;
- scale pulsing used as breathing for every character;
- camera drift used to hide absent local performance;
- poses whose silhouettes lose the held object or action target;
- material shadows that slide independently of the performer.

Use replacement poses without crossfading when the design benefits from graphic cuts. Use short overlaps only when they preserve identity and do not produce double heads, extra limbs, or floating contact.

## 5. Sound and release gates

Use paper, pin, fold, slide, impact, and environment sounds selectively. Synchronize important contacts and pose holds. Dialogue and music follow measured performance, not arbitrary scene duration.

The benchmark must prove:

1. route-readable paper/card construction;
2. at least one internal articulation or complete-state performance phrase;
3. stable pivots/support/contact;
4. an affected-object or character reaction;
5. synchronized sound or an explicitly approved silent-film design;
6. clean silhouette and material joins at delivery size.

Reject a release when the route is only a surface filter, action is presentation-only, or reused poses contradict the shot intention.
