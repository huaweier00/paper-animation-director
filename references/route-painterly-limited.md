# Painterly limited-animation route

Use this route for gongbi, watercolor, ink, silk-like, illustrated, or softly painted narrative animation whose characters are not literal articulated paper puppets.

## Contents

1. Medium honesty
2. Performance architectures
3. Painterly integration
4. Motion and timing
5. Benchmark and release gates

## 1. Medium honesty

Call the work painterly limited animation. Do not claim leather transmission, puppet rods, carved openwork, cardboard thickness, or paper joints unless they are materially present and consistently animated.

The route may borrow shadow-theatre economy—strong silhouette, stepped poses, exact holds, substitutions, musical accents—without imitating a false screen or material.

## 2. Performance architectures

Choose per shot:

- authored complete-pose replacement;
- a controlled local rig built from compatible painted parts;
- connected character/prop or actor ensemble states;
- integrated full-scene before/action/after states;
- selective local painting changes inside a stable tableau;
- deliberate still image with earned-stillness evidence.

Generate poses from intention and action phase, not a universal stock library. A close emotional performance may need gaze, head, shoulders, hands, breath, and weight changes. A broad action may need anticipation, extreme/contact, breakdown, settle, and reaction states.

## 3. Painterly integration

Match character, prop, and environment across:

- perspective and camera height;
- value hierarchy and color atmosphere;
- key-light direction and softness;
- line weight, edge sharpness, and depth falloff;
- texture scale and paint behavior;
- support, contact, cast shadow, and occlusion.

Regenerate or repaint the weakest source when joins contradict. Do not hide mismatches with one global antique LUT, grain layer, blur, bloom, or paper texture.

## 4. Motion and timing

Preserve drawing appeal during movement. Avoid rubbery mesh deformation, continuous scale breathing, symmetric easing, and whole-body drifting.

Use:

- authored pose contrast;
- shaped holds around thought;
- brief anticipation when it clarifies force or intention;
- exact contact/change frames;
- selective secondary motion tied to the primary action;
- reaction and recovery before the cut;
- camera movement only after static staging reads.

One painted pose cannot carry fear, decision, threat, comfort, travel, and farewell merely because the face remains identifiable. Register shot-specific performance state and reject incompatible reuse.

## 5. Benchmark and release gates

The benchmark must prove:

1. one emotional or physical intention changes through authored local/pose/state performance;
2. one contact, object change, or relationship change reads without captions;
3. the character remains integrated with the painted world throughout motion;
4. line, anatomy, face, hands, and edges survive pose/state changes;
5. sound gives timing and identity rather than filling dead duration;
6. presentation motion is optional rather than the main evidence.

Reject release when:

- an action-bearing actor uses only a rigid PNG root transform;
- a long narration spans an unchanged plate through several different verbs or intentions;
- pose replacement produces identity, anatomy, baseline, light, or crop jumps;
- the visual route is attractive but generic or emotionally neutral;
- the final audio expected by the story is missing.
