# Sound-action cueing

Use this reference before final timing, performance blocking, voice generation, music selection, or release. Sound is an editorial and performance authority, not a finishing layer.

## Contents

1. Audio posture
2. Cue ledger
3. Performance synchronization
4. Route-specific sound
5. Mix and release gates

## 1. Audio posture

Declare one mode in `audio-contract.json`:

- `full-performance` — dialogue/singing plus music, effects, and ambience as required;
- `dialogue-and-sound-design` — spoken performance plus effects/ambience, with optional music;
- `music-and-effects` — no spoken lines by design, but authored performance sound remains;
- `intentional-silent` — a deliberately silent film with written narrative and rhythmic rationale.

`music-only` is not a valid substitute for expected dialogue, singing, or performance sound. Shadow theatre requires `full-performance` or `dialogue-and-sound-design`.

## 2. Cue ledger

Record each cue with:

- stable ID and owner/speaker/source;
- type: dialogue, singing, percussion, music accent, effect, ambience, or intentional silence;
- measured start and duration;
- linked shot, action phase, contact, hold, reaction, transition, or caption;
- source file and content hash for final stems;
- expected presence in master and derivatives.

Preserve dry voice/singing stems. Never globally stretch speech to fit a locked picture. Refit picture to selected measured performances or record a new approved take.

## 3. Performance synchronization

Use sound to shape rather than decorate motion:

- breath or silence may precede intention;
- percussion or effect may land on entry, contact, substitution, pose hold, or exit;
- music pulse may organize procession, gait, fight, ritual, or repeated labor;
- dialogue accents may trigger gaze, hand, torso, or reaction changes;
- ambience perspective changes with location, distance, and cut;
- recovery and reaction receive time after the accent.

Do not attach one accent to every motion. Reserve emphasis for meaningful change. A visible contact without an appropriate sound response may feel weightless; a loud sound without visible cause may feel pasted on.

## 4. Route-specific sound

### Shadow theatre

Use singing/dialogue, percussion, role motifs, and instrumental pulse as part of manipulation. Bind major entries, gestures, weapon actions, substitutions, and holds to cues. A silent or music-only master fails the route.

### Cutout paper

Use tactile paper, pin, fold, slide, scrape, impact, and environment sounds selectively. Avoid turning every tiny joint change into foley noise.

### Painterly limited animation

Use voice, ambience, music, and selective effects to shape thought, action, and visual holds. Do not let narration park over an unchanged image through multiple different verbs or intentions.

## 5. Mix and release gates

Review three passes:

1. picture muted — action and relationships remain readable;
2. audio only — speaker, intention, timing, environment, and transitions remain understandable;
3. combined — picture, sound, captions, light, contact, and cut land on one semantic rhythm.

Before release:

- every expected spoken/sung line exists at the approved time;
- no line is assigned to the wrong speaker;
- dialogue, narration, and singing do not collide unintentionally;
- accents have visible causes and affected reactions;
- captions match measured performance and remain readable;
- final MP4 contains the audio streams required by the declared mode;
- music replacement or platform encoding has not removed dry stems from the final mix;
- intentional silence is explicit, bounded, and supported by performance rather than missing media.
