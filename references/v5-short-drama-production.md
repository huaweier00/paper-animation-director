# V5 narrative short-drama production

Read this reference when the target is a story-led short drama rather than a narrated illustration reel. The goal is actor-led dramatic continuity in paper-animation form: intention, interruption, decision, consequence, reaction, and payoff.

## Duration posture

- Treat the requested runtime as a posture, not a quota, unless the user gives a hard delivery limit.
- Let measured dialogue, physical action, reaction time, transitions, and ending payoff determine the cut.
- Remove repeated information and inert holds. Do not remove the beats that make cause and effect readable.
- A longer complete film is preferable to a shorter synopsis; a shorter decisive film is preferable to padded minutes.

## Eight-second hook envelope

Design the first eight seconds as one miniature dramatic movement:

| Time | Required audience gain |
|---|---|
| about 0–1s | anomaly, danger, forbidden image, or unanswered physical fact |
| by about 3s | character, claim, desire, or confident false belief |
| by about 5s | contradiction or relationship that reframes the opening fact |
| by about 8s | physical consequence, irreversible question, reaction, or forward pull into scene two |

Do not wait eight seconds for the first interesting image. Do not force the whole premise into three seconds. Judge the hook as a sequence of escalating information, not as one title card.

Mute-test the hook. It must remain legible without narration. Audio-only test it. It must create a question without describing every visible action.

## Dramatic beat ledger

For each sequence, record:

- present intention;
- obstacle or interruption;
- visible decision or involuntary reaction;
- changed physical or emotional state;
- reason for the next cut.

A clause in narration is not automatically a shot. A shot is justified by a new audience inference, performance phase, spatial reveal, contact, result, or intentionally held tension.

## Anti-PPT staging

Reject a sequence when the voice continues but the image merely remains available. During any extended shot, require a designed progression such as:

```text
notice → orient → decide → act → contact → settle → react
```

The progression may use restrained movement. Eyeline, hand pressure, breath, a prop response, light pressure, occlusion, and offscreen sound can carry a beat when they change meaning.

An unchanged plate lasting through several spoken clauses is an automatic return unless stillness is the explicit dramatic action and the frame contains evolving performance or pressure. Global zoom, parallax, particles, and camera drift do not count as actor performance.

Use three visual scales to prevent page-turn rhythm:

1. relationship wide: who affects whom and where;
2. performance medium/close: eyes, throat, hands, balance, decision;
3. consequence insert: paper lifts, water ripples, brush lands, stool breaks, light withdraws.

Cut or reframe when attention changes. Preserve eyeline, screen direction, floor, support, target, and light across the cut.

## Asset production order

Follow this order:

1. script and dramatic beat ledger;
2. scratch dialogue and measured timing;
3. disposable boards and blocking silhouettes;
4. spatial contracts and screen-direction decisions;
5. hardest actor/consequence benchmark;
6. style lock and identity/model references;
7. backgrounds with playable negative space and no baked-in movable props;
8. character performance states or connected action ensembles;
9. independent targets, props, occluders, shadows, and reaction layers;
10. formal voice, integration, render evidence, and release.

Do not generate polished scene paintings before blocking proves what must move independently. Do not bake a stool, door, window lattice, brush, or contact target into the background when the plot requires it to react.

Generate shot-specific performance art just in time. Reuse a character only when identity, facing, focal length, light, floor, support, and target remain compatible.

## Performance grammar

- Bind facing to the actor’s observed forward axis, not the filename.
- Bind travel direction, gaze target, support foot, obstacle, and exit side in the spatial/motion contract.
- For backward travel, show the searching foot before world translation.
- For a turn, stage eyes, head, shoulders, pelvis, then feet unless the action demands a different force order.
- For a drop, show attachment, release, gravity path, contact, rebound or settle, and a reaction.
- For an impact, show approach, contact frame, transferred force, prop response, settle, and aftermath.
- Keep contact-sensitive actor/prop states as one connected ensemble when separate layers would slide or miss.
- Use flight/contact alternation only inside a grounded locomotion cycle with a readable contact pose and shadow; never hold a person in a suspended pose as a scene state.

## Camera and edit grammar

- Let the camera reveal changing dramatic information; do not use it to simulate action that the actor is not performing.
- A motivated cut may be faster than a smooth transition. Use match cuts, occluder wipes, gaze cuts, sound bridges, and consequence inserts.
- Keep entry and exit vectors consistent. A left-facing actor traveling right is a contract failure unless the story explicitly stages backward motion.
- Reserve long holds for recognition, dread, choice, grief, or another active internal event. Build micro-reactions inside the hold.
- Review adjacent shots together. A good isolated frame can still create comic-book rhythm when every neighbor resets scale, light, pose, and geography.

## Dialogue, sound, and Fish Audio

- Prefer character dialogue and intentional silence over continuous explanatory narration.
- Use narration for invisible cause, compression, or reflection; never narrate an action the audience can already see.
- Preserve Fish Audio S2.1 Pro output as dry per-line stems with voice ID, model, speed, text, duration, and hash in the ledger.
- Own dramatic pauses in the picture timeline, not in a globally stretched voice file.
- Use J-cuts and L-cuts to pull the edit forward. Give brush drops, steps, impacts, breath, window pressure, and room tone their own perspective.
- Master dialogue-led social video near the chosen delivery standard while preserving headroom; verify integrated loudness, true peak, and intelligibility on phone speakers.

## V5 production gate

Before full production, the benchmark must pass muted and with sound. Before delivery, require:

- first-eight-second proof frames showing escalating information;
- a change ledger with no unexplained narration-over-static-plate interval;
- orientation and travel checks from observed pixels;
- grounded support proof for standing, retreat, turn, and run;
- contact proof for drops and impacts;
- distinct entry/contact/settle/reaction frames;
- adjacency review for cut motivation, eye trace, geography, and repeated poses;
- full-render decode, duration, dimensions, frame rate, codecs, audio streams, loudness, true peak, and final-frame inspection.

If a finished cut still feels like a slideshow, return to the earliest missing dramatic phase. More interpolation, camera drift, or transitions will not repair absent intention, contact, or consequence.
